How Much Does AI Video Cost per Second? 11 Tools Compared (2026)

AI video tools sell credits, not seconds, so it is hard to know what a clip really costs. We worked it out: here is the real price of a 5- and a 10-second clip on 11 AI video generators, using today's plan prices and each tool's own credits per clip.

Quick answer
Cheapest per second: Vidu (at 540p) and Google Veo (Veo Fast).
Best value at 720p from a dedicated video tool: Kling AI and Pika.
Most expensive: Runway (Gen-4.5) and Seedance 2.5 on InVideo.

What one minute of AI video costs

One minute of finished video is six 10-second clips. On the cheapest plans that is about $1.50 on Vidu, $3 on Google Veo, $4.80 on Kling and about $17 on Runway. In practice you rarely keep every clip you make: if you need two or three tries per clip you keep, multiply these numbers by two or three.

Why the price per second varies so much

  • Resolution. On Luma, a 1080p clip costs four times as much as 720p. On Pika, Seedance 2.5 at 1080p costs about 2.5 times as much as 720p.
  • Model. The same tool can offer cheap fast models and expensive premium ones. In Google Flow, Veo's top quality setting uses about five times the credits of Veo Fast.
  • Clip length. Costs don't always grow evenly: on Luma, a 10-second clip costs three times a 5-second one.
  • Plan size. Bigger plans give more credits per dollar: Kling goes from about $0.80 per 10-second clip on Standard to about $0.57 on Ultra+, and Hailuo from about $1.05 to $0.48.

How to pay less per second

  • Test prompts with a cheap, low-resolution model, then render your final clip with the better one.
  • Use your credits before the month ends: most plans (Runway Standard and Pro, Pika, InVideo, Krea) reset unused credits.
  • Pay yearly if you are sure: it lowers the monthly price on most tools.
