What Does a 60-Second AI Video Cost? 12 Tools Compared (2026)

AI video tools sell credits, not minutes, so it is hard to tell what one minute of finished video will cost. We took each tool's cheapest paid plan and worked out what 60 seconds of video costs, made of short clips, at the resolution and model listed. The cheapest options cost under $2 a minute; the most expensive cost more than ten times as much, and two plans don't even include enough credits for one minute.

Quick answer
Cheapest per minute: Google AI Pro with Veo Lite in Flow, about $1.50, and Vidu Standard at 540p, also about $1.50.
Cheapest at 720p with a big name: Luma Plus (Ray3.2), about $3.60, and Pika Starter (Pika 2.5), about $4.00.
Most expensive: Seedance 2.5 on Dreamina Basic and InVideo Plus, about $20 to $21. Runway Standard and Dreamina Basic don't even include enough credits for a full minute.
Last checked: 2026-11-13

What the numbers mean

The cheapest minute comes from Google and Vidu

Google AI Pro includes 1,000 Flow credits a month, and Veo's lightest setting uses about 10 credits per 8-second clip, so a minute costs about $1.50 and the plan covers more than 13 minutes. Vidu Standard is just as cheap per minute, but its Q4 model makes 540p video, which is fine for phones but soft on a big screen.

Luma and Pika: the best value at 720p

Luma's Ray3.2 at 720p and Pika 2.5 both land under $4 a minute. Luma's $30 Plus plan covers about 8 minutes a month; Pika's $10 Starter plan only 2.5 minutes, so it suits short social clips rather than longer videos.

Video with sound costs more

Kling 4.0 makes sound together with the picture, and that roughly doubles the cost compared with Kling 3.0 without sound. On Kling Standard, one minute of Kling 4.0 uses almost the whole month's 660 credits.

Some cheap plans can't make a whole minute

Runway Standard's 625 credits give about 52 seconds of Gen-4.5 video, and Dreamina Basic's 1,575 credits about 43 seconds of Seedance 2.5 at 720p. To make a full minute on those plans you have to buy extra credits or move up a plan.

Watch out

  • Retries are the real cost. Most people throw away two clips for every one they keep. The "with 3 tries" column is closer to what a finished minute costs.
  • Clip length matters. Some tools charge less per second for longer clips, others the same. A minute made of 10-second clips can be cheaper than one made of 5-second clips.
  • Editing, voice and music are extra. These numbers are for the video clips only. The project calculator adds voiceover, music and images for a whole project.
