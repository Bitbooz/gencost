Midjourney vs Firefly vs GPT Image 2.6: Cost per Image (2026)

Image generators sell plans, hours and credits, so the price of one picture is hidden. We worked out what one image costs on the cheapest paid plan of the best-known tools, and on OpenAI's new GPT Image 2.6, which you can also pay for per image.

Quick answer
Cheapest per image: OpenArt Starter, under half a cent on its simplest models, and Adobe Firefly Standard, about half a cent.
Midjourney Basic: about 1.25 cents an image (about 5 cents per grid of four).
GPT Image 2.6 on the API: about 3 to 5 cents per 1024×1024 image, with no subscription.
Last checked: 2026-11-13

What the numbers mean

Credits per image decide everything

OpenArt and Firefly look cheapest because their simplest images use about one credit each. Choose a premium model on the same plan and the cost per image can be ten or twenty times higher, as Freepik's 75 credits for a 2K Nano Banana 2 image show.

Midjourney: predictable, and unlimited from Standard

Midjourney Basic gives about 200 fast generations, each a grid of four images. From the $30 Standard plan you can make unlimited images in the slower Relax mode, which makes Midjourney the cheapest option for heavy users who can wait.

GPT Image 2.6: pay only for what you make

OpenAI's GPT Image 2.6 models, released on November 4, 2026, cost about 3 to 5 cents per 1024×1024 image on the API, depending on quality. That is more per image than the subscription tools, but with no monthly fee. Inside ChatGPT the same models are available on every plan. They can also make animated GIFs, editable SVG vectors and transparent PNGs.

Watch out

  • Unused credits disappear. On most plans monthly credits don't roll over, so an unused month raises your real cost per image.
  • Private images cost extra. On Midjourney, images are public unless you have Pro or Mega.
  • Commercial use. Free plans often don't allow commercial use. Check each tool's page before you sell or publish.
