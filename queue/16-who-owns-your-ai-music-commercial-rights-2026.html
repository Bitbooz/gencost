Who Owns Your AI Song? Commercial Rights on Suno, Udio, AIVA and More (2026)

Made a great song with AI and want to put it on YouTube, Spotify or in an ad? Then the plan you made it on matters. On most free plans you can't use the music commercially, and on some you can't even download it. Here is what each tool allows.

Quick answer
For commercial use you need a paid plan on almost every tool: Suno Pro $10/mo, Udio Standard $10/mo, Mureka Pro $8/mo.
Stable Audio is the exception: its free plan includes a commercial license.
On AIVA you only own the copyright on Pro.
Last checked: 2026-11-15

What this means

Make it on the right plan

If you plan to use a song commercially, make it while you're on a paid plan, and check the tool's terms for songs made earlier on the free plan. Mureka is clear about it: its paid plans also cover songs you made before upgrading.

Owning the copyright is not the same as a license

Most tools give you a license to use the music. AIVA is clear that you only own the full copyright on Pro. If owning the music matters, for example for a record release, read the tool's terms before you start.

Bigger companies need bigger plans

Soundraw's normal plans are for people and companies under 10 employees; bigger ones need Enterprise.
