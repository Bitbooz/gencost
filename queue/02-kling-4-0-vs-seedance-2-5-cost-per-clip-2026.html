Kling 4.0 vs Seedance 2.5: Which Is Cheaper per Clip? (2026)

Kling 4.0 from Kuaishou and Seedance 2.5 from ByteDance are the two video models most people compare this autumn. Both make clips with sound generated together with the picture, and both are sold inside many different tools. We put the price of one 5-second 720p clip side by side, platform by platform.

Quick answer
Cheapest Kling 4.0: Kling AI Standard, about $0.67 a clip.
Cheapest Seedance 2.5: CapCut Pro, about $0.60 a clip, but only in the first month; after that Higgsfield Plus, about $0.74.
On the same platform, Kling 4.0 is usually the cheaper of the two: on Luma Plus it costs about $0.80 against $1.17 for Seedance 2.5.

What the numbers mean

Buy each model at the source, or from Luma

Kling 4.0 is cheapest in Kling's own app. Seedance 2.5 is cheapest on CapCut in the first month and on Higgsfield after that. If you want both models in one subscription, Luma Plus has them side by side at fixed credits per clip, with Kling 4.0 the cheaper of the two.

Higgsfield flips the order

Higgsfield is the one platform where Seedance 2.5 is clearly cheaper than Kling 4.0: its Plus plan covers about 80 Seedance 2.5 videos, while a Kling 4.0 clip on Starter costs more than twice as much.

Google's Gemini Omni is cheaper than both, through the API

Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash, released on November 5, 2026, costs $0.07 per second at 720p on the Gemini API, about $0.35 for a 5-second clip. It is aimed at developers; in the Gemini app it is rolling out to Google AI Ultra subscribers. See Google's AI plans.

Watch out

  • Same model, different settings. Platforms don't always use the same resolution, audio and clip length for "one clip". Compare at the same settings before you decide.
  • First-month prices. CapCut and Dreamina show much lower prices for the first month. Plan your budget on the price from month two.
  • Retries. If you need three tries for a clip you keep, multiply every price above by three.
